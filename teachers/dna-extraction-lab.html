Section 01

Why Do Scientists Extract DNA?

DNA isolation is the foundation of modern biology. Before exploring how, understand why.

👆 Select at least 2 application areas below that interest you. Read each one before continuing.
Selected: 0 of 6    (choose at least 2)
✓
🔬
Forensics & Criminal Justice
DNA evidence has transformed criminal investigations — both solving crimes and exonerating the wrongly convicted.
The Innocence Project has used DNA evidence to exonerate more than 375 wrongfully convicted people in the U.S. since 1992. Extraction is the essential first step before any genetic comparison is possible.
✓
🧬
Disease Diagnosis
Genetic diseases, viral infections, and cancers can be identified by analyzing extracted DNA and RNA.
COVID-19 PCR tests work by extracting genetic material from a sample. Many cancers are now diagnosed by identifying specific mutations in extracted tumor DNA — enabling targeted therapies.
🦕
🦕
Evolutionary Biology
Comparing DNA across species reveals evolutionary relationships and ancestry in ways fossils alone cannot.
Researchers have extracted ancient DNA from Neanderthal bones, revealing that modern humans and Neanderthals interbred. Humans today carry approximately 1–4% Neanderthal DNA.
✓
🌾
Agriculture & Food Science
Crop improvement, disease resistance, and food safety testing all depend on DNA extraction and analysis.
Plant breeders use DNA extraction to select for disease-resistant traits without decades of cross-breeding. DNA testing also verifies food labeling — detecting species mislabeling in seafood and other products.
✓
🐘
Conservation Biology
Tracking genetic diversity in endangered populations helps conservation programs make better decisions.
Wildlife biologists extract DNA from non-invasive samples like hair or scat to identify individuals, track migration, and assess genetic health — all without capturing or disturbing animals.
✓
🦠
Pathogen Tracking
Sequencing pathogen DNA reveals how diseases spread, evolve, and develop drug resistance.
During the COVID-19 pandemic, genomic sequencing of extracted viral RNA allowed scientists to track variant emergence in near real-time — directly informing vaccine updates and public health decisions.

Section 06

Professional Lab vs. Strawberry Extraction

The same biology, different tools. Understanding why the methods differ is as important as knowing the steps.

Category
🏥 Professional Laboratory
🍓 Classroom Strawberry Lab
Precision
Micropipettes measure volumes to ±0.1 µL. Every reagent is weighed or volumetrically measured. Very High
Measurement is approximate — teaspoons, pinches, estimation. Low
DNA Purity
Multiple purification steps remove proteins, RNA, and contaminants. Purity verified by spectrophotometry. High
DNA is present but mixed with proteins, sugars, and other cell contents. Low
Equipment
Microcentrifuge, thermocycler, spectrophotometer, laminar flow hood, fume hood.
Plastic cups, cheesecloth, dish soap, salt, rubbing alcohol, wooden skewer.
Cost
Professional DNA extraction kits cost $200–$500 per 50 samples. Equipment costs tens of thousands. Very High
Entire lab costs under $5 of household materials. Very Low
Time
Full extraction + purification + quality check: 2–4 hours.
Visible DNA in about 15–20 minutes.
DNA Use
DNA is suitable for PCR, sequencing, cloning, diagnostic testing. Analysis-ready
DNA is visible but not suitable for most downstream analysis. Demonstration only
Reproducibility
Standardized protocols produce consistent, comparable results across labs worldwide.
Results vary with technique, temperature, and materials — each group's extraction is different.
Why Strawberries?
Strawberries are octoploid — they have 8 copies of each chromosome (most organisms have 2). This means each strawberry cell contains 4× more DNA than a typical diploid cell, making extraction dramatically more visible. Strawberries are also soft, easy to crush, and their large cells lyse readily with gentle detergent. They are scientifically ideal for classroom demonstration.
Reflection Question

Why might a classroom strawberry extraction still be scientifically valuable, even though the DNA is impure and cannot be used for sequencing or PCR?
